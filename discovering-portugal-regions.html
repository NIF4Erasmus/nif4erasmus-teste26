Discovering Portugal: A Guide to the Regions

Portugal is one of the most popular destinations for Erasmus students, and it’s not hard to understand why. Despite its relatively small size, the country offers an impressive diversity of landscapes, cultures, traditions, cuisine, and experiences.

From vibrant cities to historic villages, as well as beaches, mountains, and Atlantic islands, each region has something unique to offer, making Portugal one of Europe’s most captivating destinations. With its mild climate, relatively affordable cost of living, and great cultural diversity, the country offers unique experiences in every region.

More than just a place to study, Portugal is an invitation to discover. According to the regional division used by the European Union, Portugal is organized into seven main regions: North, Central, Lisbon, Alentejo, Algarve, Azores, and Madeira. Each one reveals a different side of Portuguese identity, and this article is designed to help you explore each of them during your stay.

North: Tradition, Nature, and Urban Life

Northern Portugal combines dynamic cities with impressive natural landscapes. The city of Porto is often the starting point, famous for its historic center, nightlife, and delicious Port wine. Other important cities include Braga, known for its religious heritage, and Guimarães, considered the birthplace of Portugal.

For nature lovers, a visit to Peneda-Gerês National Park is a must! Here you can go hiking, swim in natural waterfalls, and explore traditional villages. In the summer, coastal towns like Viana do Castelo are ideal for relaxing by the sea.

The cuisine of the North is rich and comforting, and many consider it the best in the country due to the quality and abundance of the ingredients used in its preparation. One of the region’s greatest treasures is the Douro, one of the world’s oldest demarcated wine regions. Here, the Douro River winds through valleys covered with terraced vineyards, creating a unique landscape classified as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Central: Between Mountains, Waves, History, and Tradition

Central Portugal stands out for its diversity. The city of Coimbra, home to one of the oldest universities in Europe, is especially popular among students. Aveiro, often called the “Portuguese Venice,” charms visitors with its canals and colourful architecture.

Some of Portugal’s most iconic places (and indeed of the world!) are found here. Nazaré is world-famous for its giant waves, making it a must-visit destination even for non-surfers. Fátima is one of the world’s most important religious pilgrimage sites, attracting visitors from all cultures.

If you enjoy adventure, Serra da Estrela offers hiking trails, snow in winter, and stunning landscapes. For a more historical experience, visit Óbidos, a walled medieval town, or Tomar, which is linked to the Knights Templar.

In Central Portugal, Peniche also stands out as one of Europe’s main surfing hotspots, known for its consistent waves, such as Supertubos beach. Although it is located relatively close to Lisbon, Peniche is administratively part of the Central region, which makes it an interesting example of how geographical proximity does not always match regional divisions. Beyond surfing, you can also explore the Berlengas Islands, a protected archipelago with crystal-clear waters and stunning natural landscapes, ideal for boat trips and diving.

Lisbon: Culture, Nightlife, and International Energy

The Lisbon region is the cosmopolitan heart of Portugal. The capital, Lisbon, is famous for its historic neighbourhoods, viewpoints, and vibrant cultural life. Old districts such as Alfama and Bairro Alto are perfect for exploring during the day and going out at night.

Lisbon is also an extremely cultural city: museums, concerts, festivals, and a strong connection to traditional Portuguese music, such as fado, are part of everyday life.

In the surrounding area, there are destinations you can’t miss:

  • Costa da Caparica – known for its long beaches and relaxed atmosphere
  • Ericeira – one of Europe’s best surfing reserves
  • Sintra – with palaces and fairytale-like landscapes
  • Cascais – ideal for a more sophisticated beach day
  • Mafra – home to the impressive convent and a strong historical heritage
  • Setúbal – known for its fresh fish and proximity to the Serra da Arrábida

In the Serra da Arrábida, you can find some of the most beautiful beaches in Portugal, with crystal-clear waters and protected landscapes. Lisbon is also an excellent starting point for trips across the country.

Alentejo: Tranquillity, Gastronomy, and Authenticity

Alentejo offers a slower and more authentic pace of life. The city of Évora, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, is one of its main highlights, featuring Roman ruins and beautifully preserved historic architecture.

Other places such as Monsaraz offer incredible views over the Alentejo countryside. This region is perfect for those who want to relax, taste local wines, and enjoy traditional cuisine. On the coast, beaches like those in Vila Nova de Milfontes and Zambujeira do Mar are less touristy and extremely beautiful, ideal for those seeking quieter seaside spots.

Traditionally a region strongly focused on agriculture and livestock farming, its cuisine is one of its greatest highlights, often accompanied by regional wines, making this region a true gastronomic paradise.

Algarve: Sun, Beaches, and Social Life

Algarve is the most famous region during the summer and the favourite holiday destination for Portuguese people. Towns such as Albufeira are known for their nightlife, while Lagos combines stunning beaches with history. Vilamoura is another hotspot for entertainment, with its marina, bars, and more upscale clubs.

For impressive natural landscapes, visit Sagres, at the southwestern tip of Europe.

Algarve is ideal for parties, water activities, and relaxed days in the sun. Here you can enjoy water sports, boat trips, and explore caves and cliffs, and of course, make the most of the sunshine almost all year round.

Azores: Wild Nature and Adventure

Azores are a Portuguese archipelago located in the Atlantic Ocean, made up of nine volcanic islands. They are a true paradise for nature lovers. São Miguel Island, where Ponta Delgada is located, offers volcanic lakes such as Sete Cidades and unique landscapes.

Here you can go hiking, watch whales, swim in thermal waters, and explore an almost untouched natural environment. It is a perfect destination for quieter and more unique getaways.

But the Azorean experience goes far beyond its landscapes. The cuisine is unique, with standout dishes such as “cozido das Furnas” (slow-cooked using volcanic heat), fresh fish, and high-quality local products. The close connection to nature, combined with the authenticity of the food, makes this region truly special. Exploring the Azores islands will undoubtedly be a memorable experience, given their natural beauty and their distinct character compared to the rest of Portugal’s regions.

Madeira: Subtropical Climate and Lush Landscapes

Madeira is a Portuguese archipelago, also located in the Atlantic Ocean, made up of four main islands: Madeira, Porto Santo, Desertas, and Selvagens (the latter two are uninhabited). Madeira combines nature, a pleasant climate, and unique experiences. The city of Funchal is the centre of life on the island, with markets, gardens, and cultural events.

One of the most popular activities is walking the “levadas,” which are trails that follow irrigation channels and offer spectacular views. The island is also excellent for hiking, boat trips, and exploring impressive cliffs.

But Madeira is also known for its cuisine: espetada madeirense, bolo do caco, and fresh fish are must-tries. Traditional drinks are another highlight. The famous Madeira Wine is internationally renowned, and poncha (a traditional drink made with sugarcane spirit) is an essential experience for anyone visiting the island.

New Year’s Eve in Madeira is also a unique experience, as from a high viewpoint on the island you can see multiple fireworks displays, creating a truly unique and unforgettable scene.

Conclusion

Living in Portugal as an Erasmus student is much more than attending classes, it’s an opportunity to explore a diverse country full of personality! Each region offers unique experiences, from parties and beaches to nature, history, gastronomy, and culture.

No matter where you are based, travelling around the country is easy and affordable, so make the most of it and discover everything that Portugal has to offer.

In conclusion, it is important to emphasise that this article presents only a selection of the many destinations Portugal has to offer. The country is full of unique places, each with its own charm, history, and identity. Therefore, you should not limit your travels to the locations mentioned here, as there are countless lesser-known spots that may surprise you. Depending on your interests, whether nature, culture, nightlife, or tranquillity, you will be able to find other destinations that are equally suited to what you are looking for.

Exploring Portugal is, above all, about letting yourself be guided by discovery!
